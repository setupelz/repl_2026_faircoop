Equitable cooperation explorer

Preview: the data release follows the article

This page redraws the six indicator figures of UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2026, Chapter 5, from the scenario set behind Pelz et al. (2026). Every line reaches the same carbon budget. The lines differ in who does what: the cost-optimal pathway the model starts from, the same pathway once each region must meet a fair share of the budget with unlimited finance between regions, and the same again with finance held to the lowest level the model tolerates. Pick a region to see where the pathways part ways.

How to read these figures
The two corners. Regions that have used more than their share of the budget carry a debt. They can pay it down by cutting emissions faster at home, by removing carbon at home, or by financing action in other regions. With unlimited finance (U) the physical transition matches the cost-optimal pathway and the fair share is settled by money. With finance held to the lowest level the model tolerates (L) the higher-responsibility regions cut fossil fuels faster at home and the regions with less historical responsibility gain relative to the cost-optimal pathway.
The fair-share variants. The default pair shares the budget by equal cumulative emissions per person counted from 2015 (ECPC 2015) on the SSP2 narrative. The drawer adds pairs that change the principle (equal per-capita emissions each year, CAPC), the year responsibility starts (1990 or 2025), the narrative (SSP1), the cooperation channel (novel carbon removal only), the timing (cooperation from 2040) or the discount rate (1%). Each chip adds both corners.
The same climate outcome. Two budgets, both counted from 2020 and both allowing overshoot: 800 Gt CO2 for about 2 °C at 67% likelihood and 500 Gt CO2 for 1.5 °C at 50% likelihood. The budget binds the emissions the fair-share rules cover. Land use, international shipping and aviation sit outside it, so total CO2 over 2020 to 2100, the strip below, lands within a few percent of the budget for every line. The baseline has no new climate policy and sits far off this scale.
